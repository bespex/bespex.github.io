The introduction has shown you what CodeDeck is and whether it suits you. The three parts of the manual that follow each have a job of their own, and you read them in different ways.

## The tutorial: once from start to finish

The [tutorial](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/) takes you through a complete round of work with an AI session. It begins with setting up CodeDeck and your coding agent. Then you get to know the project window, start a session and follow how it works. When it is done, you look at its changes, tidy up the Workbench and prepare the next prompt. Finally you secure the result with a commit and close the round.

The chapters build on each other. You get the most out of the tutorial if you work through it once, calmly and in order, trying out CodeDeck on a project of your own as you go. Afterwards you know every area of the window and what it is for.

A short version of it is also built into CodeDeck itself. In the CodeDeck menu behind the logo at the top of the window you'll find the entry "Tutorial: project window". In a few steps it takes you through the areas of the window, following the same round of work.

## Reference: when you want to know something exactly

The [Reference](/codedeck/manual/en/reference/) part is organized by topic and doesn't have to be read in order. Each page covers one topic completely, for example AI sessions, changes and turns, snapshots, the next prompt, the Workbench, notifications or settings. You come here when you run into a question in everyday work or want to understand a feature in more detail than the tutorial explains it.

The reference also includes the basics of Git and of working with an AI, the keyboard shortcuts and a glossary with every term. If something doesn't work the way you expect, the page [When something doesn't work](/codedeck/manual/en/reference/troubleshooting/) will help you.

## The appendix: for setup and background

The [Appendix](/codedeck/manual/en/appendix/) holds what you need only now and then. It describes the programs CodeDeck works together with, the supported coding agents, the differences between the desktop systems Wayland and X11, the commands for the command line, and how to install and update CodeDeck. You look in here mainly during setup or when something on your computer has changed.
