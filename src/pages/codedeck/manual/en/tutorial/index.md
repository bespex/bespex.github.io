## How to work through the tutorial

The tutorial follows a single **round of work**: you give an AI session a task, follow how it works, check its result, give it the next task with everything it needs for it, and at the end you secure your work with a commit. Each chapter covers one stop on this round and the part of CodeDeck that helps you there.

You learn the most when you have CodeDeck open while reading and try everything out on a small project of your own. If you have no suitable project at hand, simply create an empty folder and add it to CodeDeck. CodeDeck then offers to make it a git repository with one click. The first chapter shows how. Such a folder is enough for the first steps.

The chapters build on each other, so read them in order the first time. At the end of each chapter you find a small exercise under **Try it out**, and under **More on this** the pages where you can go deeper into a topic.

The tour "Tutorial: project window" in the CodeDeck menu goes through the same round in short form. It is a good way to refresh your memory quickly later on. The tutorial here, on the other hand, explains in more detail why something is the way it is and shows you each step one by one.

## The sample project

The pictures in the tutorial come from a sample project called `orbit-api`. In it, you work on the branch `feature/rate-limit` on a limit for how many requests a program accepts within a certain time. Several AI sessions take part: one builds the limit itself, one writes the documentation for it, and another one takes care of the configuration.

The names of the sessions, their questions and their closing words appear in the pictures as the AI wrote them. The CodeDeck interface is set to English. On your computer, everything looks a little different, depending on what you are working on and which colour scheme you have chosen.

## The stops of a round

A **turn** is everything an AI session does in response to one of your prompts, until it is finished and waits for you again. You can learn more about turns, prompts and AI sessions in the [AI basics](/codedeck/manual/en/reference/ai-basics/). The tutorial goes through these stops:

1. [Preparation](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/01-preparation/): you set up CodeDeck, connect your coding agent and add your first project.
2. [The project window](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/02-project-window/): you get to know the parts of the window.
3. [Start an AI session](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/03-start-session/): you start a session, give it a task and answer it when it asks you something.
4. [Activity](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/04-activity/): you follow what your sessions are doing.
5. [Changes](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/05-changes/): you look at what a turn changed and comment on individual lines.
6. [Workbench](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/06-workbench/): you look at what came about beside the actual project.
7. [Next prompt](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/07-next-prompt/): you give the AI what it needs for the next turn.
8. [Trail and snapshots](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/08-trail/): you see how safe your work is and go back if you need to.
9. [Files and commit](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/09-commit/): you find your way around the file tree and bring the result into git with commit and push.
10. [Closing the round](/codedeck/manual/en/tutorial/10-close-round/): you sum up the round, begin the next one and work with several sessions and projects at once.
